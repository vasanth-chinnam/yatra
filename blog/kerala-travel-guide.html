The Complete Kerala Travel Guide — God's Own Country

📅 June 2025 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ Yatra Travel Team

Kerala is unlike anywhere else in India — a narrow coastal strip sandwiched between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, draped in coconut palms, laced with 900 kilometres of backwater canals, and perfumed with cardamom, pepper and coffee. Small in size but extraordinary in diversity, Kerala packs beaches, backwaters, rainforests, hill stations, wildlife reserves and ancient temples into one compact destination.

Known as "God's Own Country" — a tagline that tourism marketing made famous but Kerala itself makes true — this southwestern state has long been India's most-visited domestic and international destination. Here's everything you need to know to plan your perfect Kerala trip.

🗓 Best Time to Visit Kerala: September to March is ideal. October–February offers perfect weather with cool temperatures and clear skies. Avoid the monsoon (June–August) for backwater trips and beaches, though the Ghats look spectacular in the rains. Onam festival (August–September) is worth planning around.

The Backwaters — Kerala's Defining Experience

Nothing defines Kerala more completely than its backwaters — a 900-kilometre network of interconnected canals, rivers, lakes and lagoons running parallel to the Arabian Sea coast. The most famous stretch is around Alleppey (Alappuzha), where overnight houseboat cruises glide through narrow waterways lined with coconut groves and rice paddies.

🚢 Alleppey Houseboat Experience

A converted rice barge (kettuvallam) fitted with bedrooms, a kitchen and a viewing deck makes for one of India's most iconic overnight experiences. Prices range from ₹8,000–₹25,000 per night depending on the houseboat's quality. Book through certified operators for the best experience. Opt for the quieter northern canals rather than the busy tourist routes near Alleppey town.

🌊 Kumarakom & Vembanad Lake

Kumarakom on Vembanad Lake is Kerala's second backwater hub — more upscale and less crowded than Alleppey. The Kumarakom Bird Sanctuary attracts Siberian storks and migratory birds from October to February. The luxury resorts here offer private jetties and water villas.

Hill Stations — The Misty Highlands

☕ Munnar — Tea Country

At 1,600 metres above sea level, Munnar is Kerala's premier hill station — a rolling landscape of emerald tea gardens, misty mornings and cool clean air that's a world away from the tropical coast below. The Eravikulam National Park protects the endangered Nilgiri Tahr and hosts the spectacular Neelakurinji flower bloom (once every 12 years, next in 2030). The Top Station viewpoint on the Kerala-Tamil Nadu border offers views across three states on clear days.

🌿 Wayanad — Spice & Forest Country

Wayanad district in northern Kerala is one of India's finest eco-tourism destinations — dense coffee and spice plantations, ancient Edakkal Caves with prehistoric rock art, and the Chembra Peak trek that rewards hikers with a heart-shaped lake at the summit. The Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary is home to elephants, gaur, and leopards.

Beaches — The Arabian Sea Coast

🏖 Kovalam

Three crescent beaches — Lighthouse Beach, Hawa Beach and Samudra Beach — make Kovalam Kerala's most developed beach resort. The iconic red-and-white lighthouse towers over the headland.

🧘 Varkala

Red laterite cliffs plunging dramatically into the sea make Varkala unlike any other Indian beach. The cliff-top promenade is lined with yoga centres, seafood restaurants and boutiques.

Wildlife — Into the Western Ghats

🐘 Periyar Tiger Reserve

Built around a beautiful artificial lake in the Cardamom Hills, Periyar is Kerala's most visited wildlife sanctuary. Boat rides on Periyar Lake offer close-up views of wild elephants, bison, deer and otters drinking at the shoreline. Guided trekking programmes through the reserve's buffer zone offer more immersive wildlife encounters.

Culture & Heritage

Kerala's cultural heritage is as rich as its natural beauty. The Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram — whose underground vaults reportedly contain treasures worth over a trillion rupees — is one of India's wealthiest and most sacred temples. Fort Kochi's Chinese fishing nets, Dutch Palace, Jewish Synagogue and Portuguese churches reflect 500 years of colonial history. Kathakali dance performances, Kalaripayattu martial arts demonstrations and traditional percussion concerts (Thrissur Pooram) showcase Kerala's extraordinary performing arts tradition.

Ayurveda — The Healing Tradition

Kerala is the birthplace of Ayurveda and home to India's finest traditional medical centres. Authentic Ayurvedic treatments — panchakarma detox programmes, therapeutic massages, herbal remedies — are available everywhere from budget ayurvedic centres to luxury resorts. A minimum 7-day Ayurvedic retreat is recommended for meaningful results.

🍽 What to Eat in Kerala: Kerala's cuisine is a spice-lover's paradise. Must-tries include karimeen (pearl spot fish) fry, prawn moilee, appam with stew, puttu and kadala curry, Kerala beef fry, and the legendary Kerala sadya — a 20-dish vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf during Onam.

Sample 7-Day Kerala Itinerary

Day 1–2: Arrive Kochi — Fort Kochi heritage walk, Chinese fishing nets, Kathakali show

Day 3: Drive to Munnar — tea garden visits, Eravikulam National Park

Day 4: Munnar to Periyar — boat ride on Periyar Lake, spice plantation tour

Day 5–6: Periyar to Alleppey — overnight houseboat cruise through the backwaters

Day 7: Alleppey to Thiruvananthapuram — Padmanabhaswamy Temple, Kovalam beach
