Ladakh Travel Guide 2025 — Everything You Need to Know

📅 June 2025 ⏱ 12 min read ✍️ Yatra Travel Team

There are places that change you, and Ladakh is one of them. At an average elevation of 3,500 metres, India's highest union territory is a stark, otherworldly landscape of barren mountains, impossibly blue lakes, and ancient Buddhist monasteries perched on clifftops that seem to defy gravity. Ladakh is equal parts adventure, spirituality, and sheer visual spectacle.

Once a remote Himalayan kingdom accessible only to intrepid travellers, Ladakh today welcomes hundreds of thousands of visitors each year — yet its vast, sparse landscape absorbs them easily. Whether you come for the motorcycle ride of a lifetime, the meditation retreats, the wildlife photography, or simply to stand on top of a 5,000-metre pass and feel impossibly small, Ladakh delivers.

⚠️ Altitude Warning:

Leh city sits at 3,500 metres. Most visitors experience some altitude sickness — headaches, nausea, fatigue. Spend your first two days in Leh resting and acclimatising. Avoid alcohol and strenuous activity. Diamox (consult a doctor) can help prevent symptoms. Never go directly from sea level to high-altitude passes without acclimatisation.

When to Visit Ladakh

Ladakh is accessible by road from May to October only — Rohtang Pass (Manali–Leh highway) and Zoji La (Srinagar–Leh highway) both close in winter due to heavy snowfall.

🌸 May–June: Best for Road Trips

Snow begins melting on the high passes. Ideal for the Manali–Leh highway drive. Pangong Tso may still have ice on its shores in May. Crowds are moderate.

☀️ July–August: Peak Season

Maximum tourist activity. All passes are open. Festivals like Hemis (July) and Ladakh Festival (September) happen in this period. Book accommodation well in advance.

🍂 September–October: Ideal Weather

Crowds thin out, skies are crystal clear, temperatures are pleasant. Best time for photography and trekking. Most passes start closing in October.

❄️ November–April: Winter Ladakh

Roads closed, most hotels shut. But the Chadar Trek — walking on the frozen Zanskar River — operates January–February and is a legendary experience for hardy adventurers.

Top Places to Visit in Ladakh

🔵 Pangong Tso — The Colour-Changing Lake

At 4,350 metres, Pangong Tso is a 134-kilometre-long lake that straddles India and China (only about 40km of it is in India). The lake's water shifts through five distinct shades of blue — turquoise, cobalt, sapphire, teal and navy — depending on the light and season. The 4–5 hour drive from Leh via Chang La pass (5,360m) is an adventure in itself. Staying overnight in a camp on the lake's south bank is a highlight of any Ladakh trip.

🐪 Nubra Valley — Sand Dunes in the Himalayas

The Nubra Valley — reached via the legendary Khardung La pass at 5,359 metres — is a surreal landscape of sand dunes and Bactrian (double-humped) camels sandwiched between snow-capped Himalayan peaks. The Diskit Monastery, perched on a rocky outcrop above the valley, houses a 32-metre statue of Maitreya Buddha overlooking the landscape. The village of Turtuk, the last Indian village before the Pakistani border, has a unique Balti culture distinct from the rest of Ladakh.

🏔️ Khardung La Pass

For years billed as the world's highest motorable road, Khardung La at 5,359 metres is a pilgrimage for every motorcyclist and road-tripper in India. The wind at the top is fierce, the views are breathtaking, and the sense of achievement is real. The army-run tea stall at the summit serving hot Maggi noodles is an institution.

🙏 Monastery Circuit — Thiksey, Hemis, Alchi

Ladakh has over 200 Buddhist monasteries (gompas). The must-visit ones are Thiksey (a 12-storey monastery resembling the Potala Palace in Lhasa), Hemis (Ladakh's largest and wealthiest gompa, famous for its June/July festival), and Alchi (Ladakh's oldest monastery, dating to the 11th century with extraordinary wall paintings). The Leh Palace — a 17th-century palace modelled on the Potala — overlooks the city from a hilltop.

🏜️ Lamayuru — The Moonland

Between Leh and Kargil, Lamayuru is famous for its "moonscape" landscape — eroded, crater-like terrain that looks like the surface of the moon. The Lamayuru Monastery, one of Ladakh's oldest, sits dramatically above this alien landscape.

🌟 Hanle — India's Dark Sky Reserve

Remote Hanle in the Changthang plateau (4,500m) is home to the Indian Astronomical Observatory and is India's first designated Dark Sky Reserve due to its high altitude, dry climate and minimal light pollution. It's a dream destination for astrophotography.

How to Reach Ladakh

By Air: Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport in Leh has daily direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai and Srinagar. Perfect for a quick trip, but requires strict 48-hour rest for altitude acclimatisation.

By Road: The Manali-Leh Highway (474km) is the more popular and spectacular route, crossing 5 mountain passes. The Srinagar-Leh Highway (418km) is a safer, more gradual climb via Kargil, better for reducing altitude sickness.

Permits & Regulations

An Inner Line Permit (ILP) is mandatory for domestic and international travellers to visit restricted areas like Pangong Tso, Nubra Valley, Turtuk, Tso Moriri and Hanle. Permits can be obtained online via the Ladakh Leh Portal or through travel agencies in Leh town.
