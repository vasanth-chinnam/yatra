Best Time to Visit India — Month by Month Guide

📅 June 2025 ⏱ 9 min read ✍️ Yatra Travel Team

India is a sub-continent rather than just a country, spanning from the freezing glacial heights of the Himalayas in the north to the tropical, spice-scented coastlines of the south. As a result, there is no single "best time to visit India" — while one region is experiencing scorching summer heat, another is locked under deep winter snow, and a third is enjoying perfect beach weather. To plan the perfect trip, you must match your destinations with the right months.

Broadly speaking, India experiences three primary seasons: **Winter (October to March)**, **Summer (April to June)**, and the **Monsoon (July to September)**. Here is our comprehensive month-by-month guide to help you decide exactly when to visit every part of India.

🗓 Quick Summary: For a classic sightseeing trip covering the Golden Triangle (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur), Rajasthan, or Kerala, plan your trip between **November and February**. For Himalayan trekking, Ladakh, and high mountain passes, plan between **June and September**.

1. The Peak Season: Winter (October to March)

This is the most popular time to visit the vast majority of India. The plains and coastal regions enjoy dry, sunny days and cool, comfortable nights.

🍁 October & November — The Autumn Awakening

October marks the transition out of the monsoon. Skies clear up, landscapes are green and lush, and temperatures begin dropping. November offers near-perfect weather across almost all of India.

  • Where to go: Rajasthan, Delhi, Agra (Taj Mahal), Varanasi, Madhya Pradesh, and Kerala backwaters. High altitude mountain regions are clear but get cold.
  • Highlights: Diwali (Festival of Lights), Pushkar Camel Fair (Rajasthan), and the start of the beach season in Goa.

❄️ December & January — The Winter Chill

The coolest months of the year. Northern India experiences chilly mornings and nights with daytime sunshine. Southern India and coastal areas have beautiful warm temperatures around 25-28°C.

  • Where to go: Goa's beaches, Kerala, Karnataka, Mumbai, and Gujarat. Ideal for tiger safaris in Ranthambore or Kanha as tigers bask in the sun. Delhi and Agra can experience morning fog.
  • Highlights: Sunburn Festival in Goa, Rann Utsav in Gujarat's salt desert, and Christmas/New Year beach celebrations.

🌸 February & March — The Golden Window

Warm, sunny days and comfortable nights. March brings the onset of spring and the warming of the plains, making it a spectacular time to travel before the summer heat hits.

  • Where to go: Hampi, Rajasthan, Taj Mahal, Rishikesh (Yoga capital), and the beaches of Goa and Kerala.
  • Highlights: Holi (Festival of Colours), Taj Mahotsav in Agra, and the International Yoga Festival in Rishikesh.

2. The Shoulder Season: Summer (April to June)

Temperatures across the plains of India (Delhi, Rajasthan, Mumbai) rise rapidly, regularly exceeding 40°C in May. However, this is the prime time to visit the Himalayas.

⛰️ April, May & June — The Himalayan Escape

While the plains swelter, the mountain states of Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, and Ladakh enter their peak season. Snow melts, high-altitude roads open, and the weather is crisp and cool.

  • Where to go: Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala, Leh-Ladakh, Munnar (South India's cool tea hills), Ooty, and Darjeeling. May/June are excellent for tiger safaris as water holes dry up, forcing tigers to emerge.
  • Highlights: Hemis Festival in Ladakh (June/July), apple picking in Himachal, and white-water rafting in Rishikesh.

3. The Off-Peak Season: Monsoon (July to September)

Monsoon winds sweep across India, bringing heavy rain, high humidity and spectacular green landscapes. It is a fantastic time for off-beat travel and budget hunters.

🌧️ July, August & September — The Green Revolution

Rains rejuvenate the landscape. Waterfalls in the Western Ghats erupt, and hill stations are draped in mist. Rains are heavy in the south and west but gradual in the north.

  • Where to go: Ladakh (which sits in a rain-shadow and gets no monsoon), Spiti Valley, Udaipur (the lake city looks magical in the rain), Valley of Flowers (Uttarakhand), and Wayanad (Kerala).
  • Highlights: Onam Festival in Kerala (August/September), snake boat races in Alleppey, and trekking through blooming alpine meadows.
