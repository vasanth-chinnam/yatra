Top 10 Places to Visit in Rajasthan — The Royal Desert State

📅 June 2025 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ Yatra Travel Team

Rajasthan is India's most regal state — a desert kingdom where every fort tells a story of Rajput valour, every palace glitters with centuries of royal opulence, and every sunset paints the sandstone a deeper shade of gold. Whether you're a first-time visitor or returning for the tenth time, Rajasthan never stops surprising you.

Spanning over 342,000 square kilometres, Rajasthan is India's largest state by area. It stretches from the Thar Desert in the west to the Aravalli hills in the east, from the pilgrimage town of Pushkar in the north to the wildlife-rich forests of Ranthambore in the south. Here are the top 10 places you absolutely must visit.

🗓 Best Time to Visit Rajasthan: October to March is ideal when temperatures are pleasant (15–25°C). Avoid April–June when temperatures can soar above 45°C. The Pushkar Camel Fair (November) and Jaisalmer Desert Festival (February) are unmissable events.

The Top 10 Places

1

Jaipur — The Pink City

Rajasthan's capital and most iconic city, Jaipur earns its "Pink City" nickname from the rose-hued sandstone buildings of the old walled city. The massive Amber Fort perched on a hilltop overlooking Maota Lake is an absolute must — arrive early to avoid crowds and watch the fort glow gold in the morning light. Don't miss Hawa Mahal's 953 tiny windows, the City Palace's royal museum, and Jantar Mantar's astronomical instruments. The bazaars of Johari Bazaar (jewellery), Bapu Bazaar (textiles) and Nehru Bazaar (juttis/footwear) make for fantastic shopping evenings.

2

Jaisalmer — The Golden City

Rising from the Thar Desert like a mirage, Jaisalmer is built entirely from golden-yellow sandstone that glows brilliantly at sunset. The living Jaisalmer Fort — one of the world's few inhabited forts — houses hotels, restaurants and families within its 800-year-old walls. The Sam Sand Dunes offer camel safaris and cultural evenings under a canopy of stars. The intricately carved Patwon Ki Haveli and Salim Singh Ki Haveli showcase the wealth of the city's merchant class.

3

Jodhpur — The Blue City

The sight of Jodhpur's old city — thousands of indigo-blue houses cascading down hillsides below the mighty Mehrangarh Fort — is one of India's most photographed views. Mehrangarh itself is arguably the finest fort in all of Rajasthan, its museum housing royal palanquins, elephant howdahs, and miniature paintings. The bustling Sardar Market near the clock tower serves some of the best makhaniya lassi and mirchi vada in the state.

4

Udaipur — The City of Lakes

Called the "Venice of the East," Udaipur is built around a series of interconnected lakes with the Aravalli hills as a backdrop. The Lake Palace — a white marble island palace in the middle of Lake Pichola — is one of the world's most romantic hotels. The enormous City Palace complex, Saheliyon Ki Bari's fountains and gardens, and the ghats of Gangaur Ghat make Udaipur endlessly photogenic. Boat rides on Pichola Lake at sunset are a bucket-list experience.

5

Ranthambore National Park

India's most famous tiger reserve sits at the confluence of the Aravalli and Vindhya hill ranges. Ranthambore's tigers are unusually bold and regularly spotted in daylight, making it one of the best places in the world for tiger sightings. The ancient Ranthambore Fort — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — stands within the park itself, creating the extraordinary sight of tigers prowling below medieval ramparts. Book safari permits well in advance, especially October–March.

6

Pushkar — The Sacred Lake Town

Built around the holy Pushkar Lake, this ancient pilgrimage town is one of India's most spiritually significant destinations. The Brahma Temple — one of the world's very few temples dedicated to Lord Brahma — draws pilgrims year-round. The famous Pushkar Camel Fair (November) transforms the town into a vast festival of camels, folk music, and colourful chaos. The ghats around the lake offer peaceful sunrise and sunset rituals.

7

Chittorgarh — Fort of Honour

Chittorgarh Fort is India's largest fort by area and its most poignant — three times sacked, its walls have witnessed the Rajput tradition of jauhar (mass self-immolation by women to avoid capture). The 180-metre Victory Tower (Vijay Stambha) and Tower of Fame (Kirti Stambha) still stand proud within the fort's vast 700-acre precinct. The romantic ruins, palace complexes and ancient temples make this a deeply moving historical experience.

8

Bikaner — The Camel Country

Often overlooked in favour of Jaisalmer, Bikaner has its own distinct charm — the magnificent Junagarh Fort has never been conquered in its 400-year history. The National Research Centre on Camel (the world's only camel research facility), the Karni Mata "Rat Temple" at nearby Deshnok, and the old merchant havelis of the city make Bikaner a fascinating off-beat destination. The Bikaner Camel Festival (January) is spectacular.

9

Kumbhalgarh — The Great Wall of India

Kumbhalgarh Fort has the world's second-longest continuous wall after the Great Wall of China — stretching 36 kilometres across the Aravalli hills. The fort's walls are wide enough for eight horses to ride abreast. Within the fort complex lie 360 temples and the birthplace of Maharana Pratap, Rajasthan's most revered warrior king. The surrounding Kumbhalgarh Wildlife Sanctuary is home to wolves, leopards and sloth bears.

10

Shekhawati — The Open-Air Art Gallery

The Shekhawati region — comprising the towns of Mandawa, Nawalgarh, Fatehpur and Jhunjhunu — is home to hundreds of ornately painted havelis built by wealthy Marwari merchants in the 18th and 19th centuries. Every wall surface is covered in vivid frescoes depicting mythological scenes, battles, trains, and even early aeroplanes. This open-air gallery is one of Rajasthan's best-kept secrets and easily explored on a day trip from Jaipur.

Practical Travel Tips

Getting around: Rajasthan has good train connections between major cities. The Palace on Wheels luxury train covers all major destinations. For flexibility, hiring a driver for 7–10 days is the best way to cover the state.

Where to stay: Rajasthan pioneered the heritage hotel concept — staying in a converted fort or haveli is an experience unique to this state. Budget options abound in every city's old town areas.

What to eat: Dal baati churma, laal maas (fiery mutton curry), ker sangri (desert bean dish), ghewar (disc-shaped sweet), and the famous Bikaner bhujia are must-tries.
